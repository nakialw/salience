# clarify — sources and lineage

Design rationale for verifying claims against retrieved peer-reviewed literature. Grounded in claim-verification, RAG-factuality, calibration/abstention, and evidence-grading research.

## Why retrieve, never recall (the core rule)

- Walters & Wilder (2023). *Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT.* Scientific Reports 13:14045. **51% of 732 citations fabricated (47–69% range); GPT-3.5 55% vs GPT-4 18%.**
- Bhattacharyya et al. (2023). *High Rates of Fabricated and Inaccurate References in ChatGPT-Generated Medical Content.* Cureus. 47% fabricated, 46% authentic-but-inaccurate, 7% accurate; >90% wrong PMIDs.
- Gravel et al. (2023). *Learning to Fake It.* Mayo Clin Proc Digit Health. 69% of references fabricated yet surface-plausible.

## Verification pipeline and labels

- Thorne et al. (2018). *FEVER.* NAACL-HLT. arXiv:1803.05355. Three-way SUPPORTED/REFUTED/NOTENOUGHINFO + required evidence.
- Wadden et al. (2020). *Fact or Fiction: Verifying Scientific Claims (SciFact).* EMNLP. Abstract-level SUPPORTS/REFUTES + rationale over biomedical literature.
- Wadden et al. (2022). *MultiVerS.* Findings of NAACL. arXiv:2112.01640. 3-way over full-document context; atomic claims much easier; retrieval is the real bottleneck.
- Wadden et al. (2022). *SciFact-Open.* Findings of EMNLP. arXiv:2210.13777. ≥15 F1 drop open-domain — temper confidence in the open setting.
- Sarrouti et al. (2021). *HealthVer.* Findings of EMNLP. Real-world health claims; real > synthetic training.
- Wang et al. (2024). *Factcheck-GPT / Factcheck-Bench.* Findings of EMNLP. Modular claim_processor → retriever → verifier.

## Retrieval-augmented factuality (mechanism)

- Asai et al. (2024). *Self-RAG.* ICLR (Oral). arXiv:2310.11511. Adaptive, on-demand retrieval.
- Gou et al. (2024). *CRITIC.* ICLR. arXiv:2305.11738. Tool-grounded verify-then-correct.
- Dhuliawala et al. (2024). *Chain-of-Verification (CoVe).* Findings of ACL. arXiv:2309.11495. Independent verification questions; don't let the claim bias the check.
- Gao et al. (2023). *RARR.* ACL. arXiv:2210.08726. Retrofit attribution; edit/flag unsupported spans.
- Gao et al. (2023). *ALCE.* EMNLP. arXiv:2305.14627. Citation quality ≠ answer correctness; ~50% incompleteness even in best models.
- Min et al. (2023). *FActScore.* EMNLP. arXiv:2305.14251. Atomic-fact precision; <2% estimator error.
- Wei et al. (2024). *SAFE / Long-form factuality.* NeurIPS. arXiv:2403.18802. Decompose→search→verify agent; 72% human agreement, >20× cheaper.

## Decomposition — selective, not reflexive

- Hu, Long & Wang (2025). *Decomposition Dilemmas.* NAACL. arXiv:2411.02400. Accuracy-vs-noise trade-off: helps weak verifiers, can burden strong ones; 4 error types.
- Song, Kim & Iyyer (2024). *VeriScore.* Findings of EMNLP. arXiv:2406.19276. Extract only *verifiable* claims.
- Wanner et al. (2024). *A Closer Look at Claim Decomposition.* *SEM. arXiv:2403.11903. Scores sensitive to decomposition method.
- Tang, Laban & Durrett (2024). *MiniCheck.* EMNLP. arXiv:2404.10774. GPT-4-level checking at 400× lower cost; decomposition often unnecessary for strong checkers.
- DyDecomp (2025). *Optimizing Decomposition for Optimal Claim Verification.* ACL. arXiv:2503.15354. Verifier-specific optimal atomicity.

## Calibration and abstention (the NEI pathway)

- Kirichenko et al. (2025). *AbstentionBench.* NeurIPS D&B. arXiv:2506.09038. Abstention unsolved; scaling doesn't help; reasoning tuning degrades it ~24%.
- Farquhar et al. (2024). *Detecting hallucinations using semantic entropy.* Nature 630:625–630. Uncertainty over meanings flags confabulation.
- Kossen et al. (2024). *Semantic Entropy Probes.* arXiv:2406.15927. Cheap uncertainty signal.
- Wen et al. (2025). *Know Your Limits: A Survey of Abstention in LLMs.* TACL. Abstention as a first-class output; risk-coverage metrics.

## Evidence quality / source hierarchy

- GRADE framework (Guyatt et al.). RCT high / observational low certainty; per-outcome 4-level grading; rate up/down by bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias.
- Quality caveat: GRADE-rated certainty is often low/very low even in systematic reviews — source type is a prior, not a guarantee.

## Efficiency (selective refinement, cheap checks)

- Shridhar et al. (2024). *ART: Ask, Refine, and Trust.* NAACL. arXiv:2311.07961. Refining everything over-refines and flips correct answers; gate when to refine. **(Shared with `tap` v1.2.)**
- MiniCheck, Self-RAG, SAFE, Semantic Entropy Probes (above) — cheap verifier default, adaptive retrieval, cheap stop signal.

## Design mapping

| clarify feature | Literature basis |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Retrieve every source; no memory verdicts | Walters & Wilder; Bhattacharyya; Gravel (fabrication rates) |
| 3-way SUPPORTS / REFUTES / NEI label | FEVER, SciFact, MultiVerS, Factcheck-GPT |
| Explicit NEI / abstain on insufficient evidence | AbstentionBench, Wen et al., semantic entropy |
| Strength tier (strong/moderate/weak/contested) | SciFact rationale design + GRADE body-of-evidence |
| Selective claim decomposition (compound only) | Hu et al.; MiniCheck; DyDecomp; FActScore/SAFE (atomicity) |
| Decontextualize sub-claims; avoid 4 error types | Hu et al. (NAACL 2025) |
| Search for evidence independently (incl. disconfirming) | CoVe; CRITIC |
| Adaptive retrieval; cache/dedupe; stop when sufficient | Self-RAG; ART |
| Selective re-check (contested/thin only) | ART (Ask/Trust) |
| GRADE evidence weighting; flag preprints | GRADE; bioRxiv/medRxiv non-peer-reviewed |
| Report conflict as Contested, not forced verdict | MultiVerS open-setting drop; AbstentionBench |
| Verified claim = canonical verifiable `tap` | tap/sources.md (Kamoi: external checks reliable) |
| Latent-compound detection (one word, two endpoints) | Hu et al. (decomposition error: meaning alteration) + live test (vitamin D incidence vs mortality) |
| Mandatory PubMed attribution + DOI links | PubMed connector terms of use (operational, not a paper) |

## Caveats carried from the literature

- Fabrication rates (47–69%) predate retrieval grounding and are GPT-3.5/4; they justify *why* grounding is required, not the post-grounding rate.
- "Nearly solved" atomic verification (MultiVerS) holds in closed-corpus, ~1k-labeled conditions; open-domain is materially harder.
- Decomposition benefit is genuinely contested (FActScore/SAFE vs MiniCheck/Hu et al.); "decompose selectively" is the synthesized resolution, not one benchmarked result.
- Cost multipliers (MiniCheck 400×, SAFE 20×) are from the originating papers — directional.
